Why a clear brief gets better work

Process · 5 min read · 12 March 2026 · By Author Name

Most disappointing projects begin with a vague request. A brief that says what you want, for whom and by when saves weeks of back and forth.

Start with the outcome, not the format. Say what should change once the work is done: more enquiries, calmer events, a clearer message.

A closer look

Then add the limits: budget, deadline and anything that cannot change. Constraints focus creative thinking instead of blocking it.

A good brief is a conversation you have once, on paper, so you do not have it ten times in person.

Three things to try

  • Share examples you like and dislike, and say why.
  • Name one decision maker.
  • Agree how feedback will be given.
